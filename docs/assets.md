# Assets and audio

The room and cat illustration was AI-generated for this project. The page shows the empty room (`room.webp`) with the cat from the original picture on top; only the arm and mallet are animated, by Google Veo.

## How the cat clip was made

- **Still picture:** the cat and the cushion are cut out of the original image (the difference from the empty room, hysteresis threshold, largest blob, holes filled) and are the same pixels in every frame. The cut-out is pulled in 2 px and feathered, which also drops a thin grey line that the original picture has around the tail. Nothing outside the moving arm is re-rendered by the video model, so it stays sharp and perfectly still. A CSS drop shadow grounds the cat on the floor. (Veo redraws whole scenes softly and shimmers when asked for a busy background, which is why it is not used for them.)
- **Animation source:** a Veo clip of the same cat on a flat blue screen. The start image was made by subtracting the empty room (`room.webp`) from the original picture and putting the cat on pure blue; Veo 3.1 (Fast, 1280×720, 24 fps, 8 s) was asked to start and end on it (first frame = last frame), to keep the head, face and body still with the mouth and eyes closed, and to move only the arm and mallet in slow, calm taps, one per second. The cat in the clip stays within about half a pixel of the original, so the clip is aligned to the picture with a single translation of under 1 px (ECC correlation 0.997 on the cat's body).
- **Moving region:** one fixed region, the same in every frame, covering everywhere the paw, handle and mallet go (the convex hull of all pixels that differ from the picture in any used frame, about 21% of the cat layer, including part of the face that the raised arm passes in front of, and widened near the arm so the thin end of the handle below the paw is included too). Inside it every frame comes entirely from the clip; a mask that changed per frame made the paw come from the still while the mallet came from the clip, which looked like a video layer sliding over a still one. The blue is keyed out (blue-dominance matte, choked, pulled in 1 px, then its contour smoothed so the upscaled 720p key has no stair steps) and the colour of every edge pixel is taken from at least 2 px inside the arm, so no blue spill or dark fringe is left; blue is also capped at green there because the picture has no bluish or lilac pixels in that region. Where the arm leaves the cat's silhouette the layer is simply transparent, so the room shows through. Thin silhouette jitter is filtered out by morphological opening, otherwise a fringe appears around the whole cat. The video's colours are matched to the picture with a per-channel gain and offset.
- **Timing:** Veo's strikes came out at one per second (every 24 source frames at 24 fps). The moments where the mallet reaches the wooden fish were measured (the first frame where the mallet and fish area matches the contact pose), and the clip was retimed with a piecewise time warp (by picking source frames) so those moments land exactly on every 2/3 s, i.e. 90 BPM. Speeding the clip up 1.5× needs no invented in-between frames: each output frame is simply the nearest source frame, so every pose is a real one (about 36 different poses per second; an earlier clip at about 120 BPM had to be slowed down and gave only about 18, and motion interpolation tore the mallet apart). The 3-strike window with the best seam (source frames 72–144) was repeated four times.
- **Frame rate:** the shipped clip was later interpolated to 120 fps with optical flow (commit `714533b`) so it looks smooth on 120 Hz ProMotion screens.
